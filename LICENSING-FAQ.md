1. What licenses does ARSIA Protocol use?

ARSIA Protocol uses different licenses for different types of materials:

- Machine-readable interoperability artifacts, including schemas, profiles,
  and test vectors:
  Apache License 2.0

- Software components, including SDKs, scripts, tools, reference
  implementations, CI/CD workflows, and automation code:
  Business Source License 1.1, converting to Mozilla Public License Version
  2.0 (MPL-2.0) after four years from each release.

2. Can I use ARSIA Protocol in production?

Yes, production use of the ARSIA Protocol software components is permitted,
provided that your use does not include offering the software, modified or
unmodified, to third parties as part of a hosted, embedded, managed,
packaged, or substantially similar product or service that competes with
Arsia Labs' paid products or services.

3. Can my company use ARSIA Protocol internally?

Yes. Internal use within your organization, including production systems, is
not considered a Competitive Offering.

For purposes of the Business Source License grant, "your organization"
includes your company, institution, or legal entity, together with entities
that control, are controlled by, or are under common control with that entity.

4. Can contractors or consultants use ARSIA Protocol for us?

Yes. Contractors, subcontractors, consultants, and service providers may use
the ARSIA Protocol software components on your behalf, solely for your
organization's internal use and subject to the license terms.

5. Can a consulting company use ARSIA Protocol for a client project?

Generally, yes, if the consulting company is using ARSIA Protocol to deliver
a custom project for a specific client and the use is for that client's
internal business purposes.

However, a consulting company may not use ARSIA Protocol to operate, sell,
or provide a hosted, managed, packaged, white-label, or substantially similar
product or service that competes with Arsia Labs' paid products or services,
unless it has a separate commercial license.

6. What is a Competitive Offering?

A Competitive Offering means a product, service, SDK, API, platform, hosted
service, managed service, packaged distribution, or substantially similar
offering, made available to third parties, that substantially overlaps with
the capabilities of Arsia Labs' paid products or services.

Examples of Competitive Offerings may include:

- a hosted ARSIA Protocol service sold to third parties;
- a managed ARSIA Protocol platform sold to third parties;
- a commercial SDK or API wrapper that substantially overlaps with Arsia
  Labs' paid SDK or API offerings;
- a rebranded or white-label distribution of ARSIA Protocol software sold
  as a competing product;
- a commercial packaged distribution of the ARSIA Protocol software that
  competes with Arsia Labs' paid products or services.

7. What uses are generally allowed?

The following uses are generally allowed, subject to the applicable license
terms:

- internal use in production;
- development;
- testing;
- evaluation;
- research;
- interoperability testing;
- conformance testing;
- non-production use;
- building an application that uses the ARSIA Protocol but does not compete
  with Arsia Labs' paid products or services;
- using schemas, profiles, and test vectors to validate compatibility with
  the ARSIA Protocol.

8. What uses require a commercial license?

You need a separate commercial license if you want to use the ARSIA Protocol
software components to provide a Competitive Offering.

Examples may include:

- offering ARSIA Protocol as a hosted service to third parties;
- offering ARSIA Protocol as a managed service to third parties;
- selling a packaged or rebranded distribution of ARSIA Protocol software;
- embedding the ARSIA Protocol software in a commercial platform that
  substantially overlaps with Arsia Labs' paid products or services;
- providing a paid SDK, API, platform, or service that competes with Arsia
  Labs' paid products or services.

Your independent implementation may be licensed under any license of your
choice, subject to any separate rights, obligations, or agreements that may

Yes, subject to the Business Source License 1.1.

Before the Change Date, the software code is under BSL 1.1. You may copy,
modify, create derivative works, redistribute, and make non-production use

After the Change Date, the relevant version converts to MPL-2.0.

13. What happens after four years?

Each version of the ARSIA Protocol software components converts to the
Mozilla Public License Version 2.0 (MPL-2.0), without the Exhibit B
"Incompatible With Secondary Licenses" notice, four years after that version's
release.

After conversion, the MPL-2.0 terms apply to that version.

14. Does the BSL 1.1 make ARSIA Protocol open source today?

No. The Business Source License 1.1 is not an open source license. However,
the relevant software components eventually become available under an open
source license, MPL-2.0, after the Change Date.
